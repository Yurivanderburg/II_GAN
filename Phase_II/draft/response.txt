Comments to author:
The manuscript explores a conditional generative model for reconstructing simulated images of fast-rotating stars from sparsely sampled intensity-interferometry data. I found the central motivation appealing. If such a method can be validated against realistic II data, it could provide a useful complement to parametric modelling and may ultimately be relevant to stellar distortions and asymmetries arising from rotation, tides, companions, or magnetic activity. The present study is therefore a worthwhile first step. My comments below are essentially around reconciliating the github repository with the actual paper text. There is as well more minor recommendations or remarks.

Response: We sincerely thank the referee for the encouraging assessment of our work and for recognizing the potential of the proposed conditional generative model for reconstructing stellar surface images from sparsely sampled intensity-interferometry (II) data. We particularly appreciate the referee's observation that the method could provide a useful complement to parametric modelling and may have broader applications to stellar distortions and asymmetries arising from rotation, tides, companions, and magnetic activity.

We also appreciate the referee's careful effort in comparing the GitHub repository with the manuscript. We have thoroughly reviewed the manuscript and the accompanying GitHub repository to ensure consistency between the methodology, equations, descriptions, and the publicly available implementation. We have addressed the specific discrepancies and minor issues raised by the referee and made the corresponding corrections and clarifications in the revised manuscript and repository.


Important issues.

1. Relationship between the paper and the public code. Could the authors clarify which configuration produced the reported 6- and 9-IACT results? The manuscript’s abstract, figures, and results consistently discuss N_T = 6 and N_T = 9, whereas Table 1 gives “Number of Telescope: 4”. The public repository linked through Zenodo contains a four-telescope, VERITAS-like configuration (generate_baseline.py and generate_pspec.py) and supplied loss records for two, three, and four telescopes, but not six or nine. This may well be an incomplete release or a simplified example rather than the exact analysis code. It would be very helpful if the authors could reconcile Table 1 and provide the telescope positions, observing setup, and scripts or configuration files used for the two reported cases.

Response: We thank the referee for identifying this inconsistency. The value “4” in Table 1 was retained from an earlier configuration and does not correspond to the configurations used for the reported results. We have corrected Table 1 to reflect the N_T=6 and N_T=9 configurations. We have also updated the public repository to include the corresponding telescope configurations, observing setup, and relevant configuration files used for generating the results presented in the manuscript. These updates have been made to ensure consistency between the manuscript and the publicly available code and to improve the reproducibility of the reported results. We thank the referee for bringing this issue to our attention.


2. Noise model and II realism. The manuscript usefully develops the II SNR expression in Section 3.2. Could the authors explain how this expression enters the simulations? In the public code, salt-and-pepper perturbations seems to be applied to the stellar image before its FFT, and an additional perturbation is applied in the discriminator; this creates a noisy training input, but it does not appear to model uncertainty in a photon-correlation measurement at a stated per-baseline SNR. It would strengthen the physical interpretation to show results across a small set of realistic II SNR/noise conditions, or alternatively to frame the present results explicitly as a noiseless or phenomenologically perturbed proof of concept. The latter would still be useful.

3. Evaluation protocol. Could the authors clarify the handling of the inherent 180-degree ambiguity? In the public code, optional_rotation() compares a prediction and its 180-degree rotation to the ground truth using MSE and retains the closer one before saving output images; the moment script then reads those saved predictions. If this is the pipeline behind Fig. 10 and Table 2, the choice is understandable as a way to account for the physical degeneracy, but it should be described clearly because it uses the target at test time. It may also be useful to report a symmetry-aware metric, or both orientations, together with the number of test examples, uncertainties, and the image scale/normalization needed to interpret Table 2.

4. Synthetic stellar family and scope of the conclusion. The benchmark family is a useful controlled starting point. The public code implements a shaded projected ellipse with intensity (4 + |cos r_s|)/5, while the manuscript describes a linear gravity-darkening dependence and cites the classical gravity-darkening literature. Could the authors state the adopted brightness law explicitly and explain its physical relation to the cited models? This would help readers understand the scope of the test. The current data set contains smooth objects without spots, granulation, limb-darkening structure, or tidal deformation, so it may be prudent to distinguish the demonstrated fast-rotator benchmark from the interesting longer-term prospect of recovering more complex surface features. The broader scientific motivation could in fact be emphasized more strongly in this discussion.

Response:

5. Model description, baselines, and reported parameters. Section 4 describes G(z/y) with a latent variable, while the public implementation appears to use a deterministic U-Net G(y) with inference-time dropout. Could the authors clarify whether the latent-variable formulation is intended as a general cGAN description or as the exact model used here? Since the abstract notes that this particular synthetic problem might be addressed through parameter fitting, would a small comparison with a parameter-fit or an L1-only U-Net baseline help establish the role of the adversarial component? Likewise, Section 6.3 introduces position angle, semi-major and semi-minor axes, and eccentricity, but does not report their recovered values; reporting size and oblateness errors could connect the validation more directly to the stated science goal.

Response:


6. Fourier-plane registration and the 6T/9T comparison. Could the authors clarify how the angular scale of the simulated source image is mapped to the (u, v) coordinates sampled by the telescope arrays? A brief illustration, for one simulated source, that the sparse input pattern corresponds to the intended Fourier-plane samples would help readers assess and reproduce the forward model. Finally, Table 2 shows smaller scatter for N6 than N9 in every listed metric. Since the two configurations differ both in telescope number and array geometry, could the discussion state this comparison somewhat cautiously? The present comparison alone may not isolate the relative roles of coverage and baseline number.

Minor points.
